# Threadline — T-shirt store on Google Apps Script + Google Sheets

A complete, lightweight clothing store:

- **Customers** browse products, search and filter, pick colours and sizes, use a cart that survives page reloads, and reserve an order **without paying online**. They get a five-digit order reference such as `#04729`.
- **You (the owner)** manage products in a Google Sheet, receive every order as a new row in the same spreadsheet, get an email for each order, and update order statuses by hand.

No servers, databases or paid services. Everything runs on your Google account.

## Step 1 — Create the spreadsheet

1. Go to <https://sheets.google.com> and create a **blank spreadsheet**. Name it e.g. `Threadline Store`.
2. The store uses **exactly two tabs**: `Products` and `Orders`. You can create them by hand (below) or let the script do it for you in Step 2.6 (`setupSpreadsheet`).

### Products tab

**One row per product colour.** Rows that share a **Product ID** are one product; each row is one colour of it, with its own image and its own size availability.

Rename the first tab to `Products` and put these headers in row 1:

| A | B | C | D | E | F | G | H | I | J | K | L |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Product ID | Name | Description | Price | Category | Color | Image Link | S | M | L | XL | XXL |

Example:

| Product ID | Name | Description | Price | Category | Color | Image Link | S | M | L | XL | XXL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P001 | Classic Hoodie | Cotton hoodie | 899 | Hoodies | Red | https://…/red.jpg | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| P001 | Classic Hoodie | Cotton hoodie | 899 | Hoodies | Blue | https://…/blue.jpg | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| P001 | Classic Hoodie | Cotton hoodie | 899 | Hoodies | Green | https://…/green.jpg | 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| P002 | Printed T-Shirt | Printed cotton shirt | 449 | Printed | Black | https://…/black.jpg | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |

This shows **one** "Classic Hoodie" card with three colour bubbles. In Red, sizes S, M and XXL can be ordered; L and XL show as crossed out.

The script finds columns by their **header names**, so you may reorder the columns or add your own extra columns (e.g. "Notes"); they're ignored. The 12 headers above must all exist (upper/lower case doesn't matter).

Rules for each column:

- **Product ID**: any short text, e.g. `P001`. Every colour row of the same product uses the **same** ID. Pick IDs once and never reuse them for a different product: carts and orders refer to products by ID. Upper/lower case and surrounding spaces are ignored (`p001` = `P001`).
- **Name, Description, Price, Category**: must be **identical on every row of the same Product ID**. If they differ (e.g. you changed the price on one colour only), the **whole product is hidden** until the rows match again. The store never guesses which price is right. `checkCatalog` tells you exactly which row differs.
- **Price**: a plain number such as `899` or `29.99` (`29,99`, `EGP 299` and `1,299.00` also work).
- **Category**: one of `Hoodies`, `Shirt`, `Tshirts`, `Printed`, `Classic` (case and punctuation don't matter: `T-Shirts` counts as `Tshirts`).
- **Color**: the colour name shown to customers, e.g. `Red`, `Navy`, `Heather Grey`. Each colour may appear **only once per Product ID**; a duplicated colour is hidden until you remove the extra row. Bubbles use the colour name directly (any CSS colour name works). Common clothing names like Burgundy, Charcoal, Cream, Sand, Mustard and Heather Grey are built in (`COLOR_SWATCHES` in `Products.gs`). Unknown names show a neutral striped bubble.
- **Image Link**: a public `https://` image of the product **in this colour**. Customers see it when they pick that colour. Google Drive share links are converted automatically. If it's empty or broken, a placeholder is shown.
- **Image Link 2** and **Image Link 3** (optional columns, e.g. right after Image Link): extra pictures of this colour, such as the back and the side. They appear only on the product page (the popup), where a small thumbnail per picture under the main image switches between them (the one showing is highlighted); swiping and the enlarged view cycle through them too. The shop grid, cart, checkout and emails always use Image Link. Leave a cell empty, or leave a column out, for fewer pictures; an empty Image Link 2 with a filled Image Link 3 simply shows two pictures.
- **S, M, L, XL, XXL**: `1` = this size is available in this colour, `0` = not available. **An empty cell counts as 0.** Anything else (`2`, `yes`, a checkbox) is treated as **not available** and reported by `checkCatalog`. These are on/off switches, **not stock quantities**.

How availability works:

- A **size** with `0` is shown crossed out and can't be selected.
- A **colour** whose five size cells are all `0` is still shown (its bubble is struck through, its image still appears), with an "Out of Stock" badge and **Add to Cart** disabled.
- A **product** whose every colour is all `0` stays visible but can't be purchased.

#### Stock is managed by hand

The website **never changes the size cells**. A reservation doesn't reduce stock. When a colour/size runs out, change its cell to `0` yourself, and back to `1` when it's restocked. Because these are on/off flags, the store can't know how many pieces you have. Several customers can reserve the last piece of the same colour and size before you set it to `0`. You confirm every reservation by email, so you can sort this out then.

### Orders tab

Add a second tab (the **+** at the bottom left), name it `Orders`, and put these headers in row 1:

| A | B | C | D | E | F | G | H | I |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Timestamp | Order ID | Customer Name | Phone | Email | Shipping Address | Cart Items | Total Price | Order Status |

Leave the rest empty. The website appends one row per order. Don't rename or reorder these columns: the script checks them before it saves an order.

## Step 2 — Create the Apps Script project

1. In the spreadsheet, open **Extensions → Apps Script**. A new project opens that is **bound** to this spreadsheet. Rename it (top left) to `Threadline Store`.
2. **Script files (`.gs`)**: the editor starts with `Code.gs`. Replace its contents with `src/Code.gs`. Then, for each of `Config`, `Products`, `Orders` and `Email`, click **+ → Script**, type the name **without** `.gs`, and paste the matching file from `src/`.
3. **HTML files**: for each of `Index`, `Styles`, `Logo`, `Logic`, `Api` and `Scripts`, click **+ → HTML**, type the name **without** `.html`, and paste the file contents.
   File names must match exactly (case-sensitive), because `Index.html` includes the others by name.
4. **Manifest**: open **Project Settings** (gear icon), tick **Show "appsscript.json" manifest file in editor**, go back to the editor, open `appsscript.json` and replace its contents with `src/appsscript.json`. Change `timeZone` if you're not in Ireland (e.g. `Europe/Berlin`, `America/New_York`). Order timestamps are shown in the **spreadsheet's** time zone, so also check **File → Settings → Time zone** in the spreadsheet.
5. **Script Properties** (private settings): in **Project Settings → Script Properties**, click **Add script property**:

   | Property | Required? | Value |
   |---|---|---|
   | `NOTIFICATION_EMAIL` | Recommended | The address that should receive order emails. If missing, emails go to the account that owns the script. |
   | `SPREADSHEET_ID` | Only if the script is **not** bound to the spreadsheet | The long ID from the spreadsheet URL: `https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/<THIS PART>/edit` |
   | `WEBHOOK_SECRET` | For the Cloudflare site | When set, `doPost` refuses orders without it. The Cloudflare Worker sends it server-side (same value as their `WEBHOOK_SECRET` secret, see [CLOUDFLARE.md](CLOUDFLARE.md)). The Apps Script website does not use `doPost`. |

6. **Create or check the tabs**: in the editor, choose `setupSpreadsheet` in the function dropdown and click **Run**. The first run asks for authorization (see the note below). This function:
   - creates `Products` and `Orders` with the exact headers if they're missing (an empty first tab is reused as `Products`),
   - adds example rows (two products, four colours) **only if `Products` is completely empty**. A tab in the old format is never touched,
   - formats `Order ID` as plain text (keeps leading zeros) and adds a status dropdown to `Order Status`.

   It never deletes or overwrites existing data and is safe to run again.
7. Optional checks, also run from the function dropdown:
   - `checkCatalog`: lists products and colours the website is hiding and why (rows that disagree, duplicate colours, bad prices, invalid size values, bad image links). See **Execution log**.
   - `previewProductsMigration`: read-only draft conversion of an old-format Products tab (see [Migrating the Products tab](#migrating-the-products-tab)).
   - `sendTestEmail`: sends a sample order email to `NOTIFICATION_EMAIL` and shows your remaining daily email quota.

> **Authorization prompt.** The first time you run a function, Google asks for permission to *see, edit… your spreadsheets*, *send email as you* and *see your email address*. Because this is your own unpublished script, Google shows **"Google hasn't verified this app"**: click **Advanced → Go to Threadline Store (unsafe) → Allow**. Only you see this screen; customers never do.

> The owner utilities (`setupSpreadsheet`, `checkCatalog`, `previewProductsMigration`, `sendTestEmail`) refuse to run unless the person running them is the script owner, so website visitors can't trigger them.

## Step 3 — Deploy the website

1. In the Apps Script editor, click **Deploy → New deployment**.
2. Click the gear next to **Select type** and choose **Web app**.
3. Fill in:
   - **Description**: `v1`
   - **Execute as**: **Me (your@gmail.com)**. The script runs with *your* permissions, so customers can read the catalog and save orders without having access to the spreadsheet.
   - **Who has access**: **Anyone**. This lets customers use the site without a Google login.
     *Google Workspace accounts:* your admin may only allow "Anyone within <your domain>". In that case, deploy from a personal Gmail account or ask the admin to allow external access.
4. Click **Deploy** and authorize if asked.
5. Copy the **Web app URL**. It looks like `https://script.google.com/macros/s/AKfy…/exec`. **This is your store's public address.**

### Updating the website after code changes

Product, price, stock and size changes in the spreadsheet **don't** need a redeploy; they show up on the next page load.

After changing **code**:

1. **Deploy → Manage deployments**.
2. Select your deployment, click the **pencil (Edit)**.
3. **Version → New version**, then **Deploy**.

This keeps the **same URL**. Avoid "New deployment" for updates, because that creates a new URL.

To try code changes before customers see them, use **Deploy → Test deployments**. That URL (ending in `/dev`) always runs the latest saved code but only works for you.

### Optional: deploy from the command line with clasp

```bash
npm install -g @google/clasp
clasp login
cp .clasp.json.example .clasp.json     # put your Script ID (Project Settings → IDs) in it
clasp push                              # uploads everything in src/
clasp deploy -i <DEPLOYMENT_ID> -d "v2" # updates the existing web app URL
```

### Manual checklist on the live site (needs your Google account)

Open the web app URL in a private/incognito window, so you test as an anonymous customer.

**Products**
- [ ] Products from the sheet appear, with images, prices and "In stock / Out of stock".
- [ ] Tick **Hoodies**: only hoodies show. Tick **Printed** too: both categories show.
- [ ] Type in **Search**: matches on names and descriptions. Combine it with a category.
- [ ] **Reset Filters** clears everything. A search with no matches shows the friendly empty state.
- [ ] Each Product ID appears as **one** card with one bubble per colour row.
- [ ] Clicking a bubble on a card changes that card's image immediately, without opening the popup. Other cards don't change.
- [ ] **View Product** opens with the colour you picked on the card. Switching colours there changes the big image, the "Color:" label and which sizes are crossed out.
- [ ] Sizes with `0` in the sheet are crossed out and can't be picked. A colour with all `0` shows "Out of Stock" and **Add to Cart** stays disabled.

**Cart**
- [ ] **Add to Cart** says "Select a size" and stays disabled until you pick an available size.
- [ ] Adding an item updates the cart counter and shows an "Added to cart" message.
- [ ] Add Classic Hoodie Red M, Blue M and Red L: three separate lines, each with **its colour's image**. Adding Red M again increases that line.
- [ ] **+**, **−** and **Remove** work, and totals update.
- [ ] Refresh the page: the cart is still there.

**Checkout**
- [ ] Submitting an empty form shows errors next to each field.
- [ ] `jane@` (email) and `123` (phone) are rejected.
- [ ] Double-clicking **Reserve Order** creates only one order (the button disables and shows "Reserving your order…").
- [ ] With valid details, the thank-you screen shows **Order Reference Number: #NNNNN**, and the cart is now empty.

**Backend**
- [ ] The `Orders` tab has a new row: timestamp, the same 5-digit ID (leading zeros kept), customer details, cart items, total, `Pending`.
- [ ] `NOTIFICATION_EMAIL` received **"New T-Shirt Order #NNNNN"** with all details. Replying to it goes to the customer.
- [ ] Change a product's price in the sheet while it's in your cart, then go to checkout. The cart shows the updated price before you can reserve.
- [ ] With checkout open, set the colour/size you're ordering to `0` in the sheet, then press **Reserve Order**. The order is refused with a clear message, the line is removed from the cart, and no row is added.
- [ ] After a successful reservation, the size cells in `Products` are **unchanged**.
- [ ] The **Cart Items** cell looks like `P001 | Classic Hoodie | Blue | L | Qty: 2` (one line per item).
- [ ] Type `=1+1` as the first line of the address. The sheet shows the literal text `=1+1`, not `2`.

## Step 5 — Maintain the store

All product management happens in the `Products` tab. Changes appear the next time a customer loads the page; there's no need to redeploy.

| Task | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Add a product** | Pick a new Product ID (e.g. `P009`) and add one row per colour, with Name/Description/Price/Category identical on each row. |
| **Add a colour** | Add a row with the product's existing ID, the same Name/Description/Price/Category, the new Color, its Image Link and its 1/0 sizes. |
| **Remove a colour** | Delete that row (or set all its sizes to `0` to show it as sold out). |
| **Remove a product** | Delete all of its rows, or set every size to `0` to keep showing it as sold out. |
| **Change a price** | Change **Price on every row** of that Product ID. While the rows disagree, the product is hidden. Orders are always charged at the sheet price, and customers with it in their cart see the new price at checkout. |
| **A size sells out / is restocked** | Set that colour's size cell to `0` / `1`. Nothing changes automatically when orders come in. |
| **Rename a product** | Change **Name** on every row. The Product ID stays, so carts keep working. |
| **Check for mistakes** | Run `checkCatalog` in the Apps Script editor. It lists hidden products and colours, invalid size values and bad image links. |
| **Manage orders** | Change **Order Status** in the `Orders` tab (dropdown: Pending, Confirmed, Paid, Shipped, Delivered, Cancelled). The site never changes statuses after creating the row. |

### Upload and link product images

Each colour row has its own **Image Link**, a public `https://` image URL of the product in that colour. Options:

- **Google Drive** (easiest):
  1. Upload the photo to Drive.
  2. Right-click → **Share** → General access: **Anyone with the link** (Viewer).
  3. **Copy link** and paste it into **Image Link**. Links like `https://drive.google.com/file/d/FILE_ID/view?usp=sharing` are converted automatically to an image URL (`https://drive.google.com/thumbnail?id=FILE_ID&sz=w1000`).
  Drive isn't built for image hosting and can be slow or throttled under heavy traffic.
- **An image host or your own website**: any direct link ending in `.jpg`, `.png` or `.webp` works and is usually faster.

Tips: use portrait images with a **4:5** aspect ratio (e.g. 1000×1250 px) and keep each file under ~300 KB. Images are cropped to fill the card (`object-fit: cover`).

### Change branding, colours, currency or categories

**Logo:** replace `the_red.jpeg` (or pass another image path), run `python3 tools/build_logo.py`, and paste the new `src/Logo.html` into the Apps Script `Logo` file. Apps Script can't serve image files, so the logo is embedded in that file.

Edit `Config.gs` (`STORE_NAME`, `STORE_TAGLINE`, `THEME`, `CURRENCY`, `LOCALE`, `CATEGORIES`, `NAV_LINKS`, limits), then **update the deployment** (Step 3, "Updating the website").

**Tab icon (favicon):** Apps Script ignores icons set in the HTML, so the icon comes from `FAVICON_URL` in `Config.gs`: a public `https://` link to a `.png` (e.g. `assets/favicon.png`, a square version of the logo, hosted like the background files). Leave it empty for Google's default icon.

**Colours:** the six `THEME` colours in `Config.gs` override the same colours in `Styles.html` when the page loads, so change them in `Config.gs`.

### Animated background

The page has one animated GIF behind everything: a landscape GIF on screens wider than `MOBILE_BREAKPOINT` (768 px) and a portrait GIF on narrower ones. It fills the screen without stretching (cropping the edges as needed, like `background-size: cover`), stays put while you scroll, and never blocks clicks. Content sits on slightly see-through dark panels so text stays readable.

The files live in `assets/`:

| File | Used for |
|---|---|
| `background-desktop.gif` / `background-mobile.gif` | the animation |
| `background-desktop-static.jpg` / `background-mobile-static.jpg` | a still shown at once while the GIF downloads, to visitors who ask their device for reduced motion, and to visitors with data saver on |

Only the files for the visitor's screen size are downloaded, and the GIF starts downloading after the page has loaded. If an image fails, the still or the plain `THEME.background` colour stays and the shop keeps working.

**To change the GIFs:** replace the two `.gif` files in `assets/` (keep the names), then run `python3 tools/build_backgrounds.py` to remake the stills.

**On the live site:** Apps Script can't serve image files, and large GIFs must not be embedded in the code. So host the four files somewhere public over `https://` and paste their links into `BACKGROUND` in `Config.gs`:

```js
BACKGROUND: {
  DESKTOP_GIF: 'https://…/background-desktop.gif',
  MOBILE_GIF: 'https://…/background-mobile.gif',
  DESKTOP_STATIC: 'https://…/background-desktop-static.jpg',
  MOBILE_STATIC: 'https://…/background-mobile-static.jpg',
  MOBILE_BREAKPOINT: 768
},
```

One free option: put the `assets/` folder in a **public** GitHub repository and use jsDelivr links, e.g. `https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/YOUR-USER/YOUR-REPO@main/assets/background-desktop.gif`. Open each link in a browser first: it must show the image itself (animated, for the GIFs), not a web page. Google Drive share links usually don't work here (they open a viewer page or return a still thumbnail). Links that aren't `https://` are ignored. With all four left empty the site simply uses the plain background colour. After editing, update the deployment (Step 3).

Each GIF is about 11–12 MB, which is heavy on mobile data and slow on weak connections (visitors see the still until it arrives). Optimising the GIFs (fewer frames, fewer colours, lossy compression) makes them load much faster.

## How orders work

1. The browser validates the form and the cart, disables **Reserve Order** and shows a spinner.
2. It sends `{ customer, items: [{ productId, color, size, quantity, unitPrice }], idempotencyKey }` through `StoreApi.submitOrder`.
3. The server (`processOrder_` in `Orders.gs`):
   1. validates every field again: lengths, formats, control characters, quantities (1–10 per line), at most 20 lines and 50 items;
   2. returns the stored result if this `idempotencyKey` was already processed;
   3. takes the **script lock** (`LockService`), so concurrent orders are processed one at a time;
   4. checks again, now holding the lock, for a duplicate that finished meanwhile;
   5. applies the rate limits;
   6. reads the **current** catalog from the sheet. For each item it looks up the Product ID, then that product's colour row, then that row's size cell, which must be `1`. Anything else (unknown ID, unknown colour, size `0`/empty, a different price than the customer saw) refuses the order with `CART_CHANGED`, and the website updates the cart and asks the customer to review it. **Stock cells are never changed**;
   7. calculates the total in whole cents from **sheet prices only**;
   8. picks a random unused 5-digit ID (`00000`–`99999`), checked against every existing ID;
   9. writes the row (Cart Items: one line per item, `P001 | Classic Hoodie | Blue | L | Qty: 2`), then caches the result under the idempotency key, then releases the lock;
   10. sends the owner email. If that fails, the order **still succeeds**: the failure is written as a **note on the Order ID cell** (hover to see it) and to the Apps Script execution log, so no columns change.
4. The browser shows the thank-you screen and clears the cart **only** after a success response with a valid order ID.

**Lost responses.** If the connection drops or the server takes too long, the outcome is unknown. The cart is kept, and the customer is told to press **Reserve Order** again *without changing anything*. The browser stores the idempotency key with the cart, even across reloads, so a retry returns the original order instead of creating a second one. The adapter also retries once automatically with the same key.

Server responses:

```json
{ "success": true,  "orderId": "04729", "total": 79.97, "currency": "EGP", "message": "Order reserved successfully" }
{ "success": false, "code": "CART_CHANGED", "message": "\"Classic Hoodie\" in Red, size L is out of stock.", "problems": [ … ] }
{ "success": false, "code": "VALIDATION", "message": "Please correct the highlighted fields.", "fieldErrors": { "email": "…" } }
```

Other codes: `EMPTY_CART`, `INVALID_CART`, `INVALID_REQUEST`, `RATE_LIMITED`, `BUSY` (retryable), `SERVER_ERROR` (retryable, nothing saved), `REJECTED` (honeypot), `UNAUTHORIZED` (webhook secret).

## Security, spam and abuse

What's implemented:

- **Server-side validation of everything.** Browser checks are only for convenience.
- **Server-side prices.** Totals come from the sheet; a tampered price is refused.
- **Formula injection blocked.** Every customer-supplied cell is written with a leading apostrophe, which Sheets treats as a "this is text" marker and hides. `=IMPORTXML(…)`, `+…`, `-…` and `@…` are stored as plain text. This also keeps phone numbers like `0871234567` from losing the leading zero.
- **HTML escaping** of all customer text in the notification email.
- **Locking** (`LockService`) around ID generation and the row write.
- **Duplicate protection**: an idempotency key per order (CacheService, 6 hours), a double-click guard, and a disabled button while sending.
- **Rate limits**: at most 5 orders per hour per email address and 60 per hour in total (configurable).
- **Honeypot field**: a hidden "website" input that people never see but simple bots fill in.
- **Private settings stay private**: only `getPublicConfig_()` reaches the browser. Functions ending in `_` can't be called through `google.script.run`, and the owner utilities check that the caller is the owner.
- **Read-only catalog**: no code path writes to `Products`, except `setupSpreadsheet` filling an *empty* tab when the owner runs it.

What's **not** guaranteed: the web app is a public endpoint, and **no public endpoint is completely protected from automated abuse**. A determined attacker can rotate email addresses, skip the honeypot and stay under the rate limits. The global hourly cap limits the damage (spam rows, email quota), but it can also block real customers during an attack.

If abuse happens:

- Lower `RATE_LIMITS` in `Config.gs` and update the deployment.
- Temporarily set **Who has access** to *Only myself* (or archive the deployment) to stop all orders.
- Delete spam rows from `Orders` (that's safe).
- For stronger protection, add a CAPTCHA (e.g. Cloudflare Turnstile) once the frontend moves to its own domain, and verify the token in `processOrder_` with `UrlFetchApp`.

## Migrating the Products tab

Earlier versions used **one row per product**:

| Name | Price | Image Link | Description | Category | Sizes Available | In Stock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The current version needs **one row per product colour** ([Products tab](#products-tab)). Until you convert the tab, the website shows "We couldn't load the products", and `checkCatalog` / the execution log say the tab is still in the old format. **Nothing converts your sheet automatically, and the Orders tab and its history are never touched.**

1. **Back up first**: **File → Make a copy** of the spreadsheet (or right-click the `Products` tab → **Duplicate**, and delete the duplicate once you're done, because the store expects only two tabs).
2. **Get a draft of the new rows (optional, read-only)**: in the Apps Script editor, run `previewProductsMigration`. It **doesn't change anything**. It prints CSV text in the **Execution log**:
   - every old product gets an ID `P001`, `P002`, … in row order,
   - one row with colour **`Standard`** and the product's existing image,
   - each size column is `1` if the size was listed in *Sizes Available* **and** *In Stock* was Yes; otherwise `0`,
   - notes about sizes that have no column any more (e.g. `XS`).
3. **Build the new tab**: clear the `Products` tab, paste the CSV (**Edit → Paste special → Paste values only**, then **Data → Split text to columns** if needed), or type the rows by hand.
4. **Assign colours**: rename `Standard` to the real colour (e.g. `Black`). For every extra colour, copy the row, keep the **same Product ID, Name, Description, Price and Category**, and change **Color**, **Image Link** and the size `1`/`0` values.
5. **Images**: paste each colour's own photo link into its row. The old image is kept on the first row, so nothing is lost.
6. **Stock**: enter `1` for each size you can sell in that colour and `0` (or leave empty) for the others.
7. **Check**: run `checkCatalog`. It reports duplicate Product ID + Color rows, rows that disagree on Name/Description/Price/Category, invalid size values and invalid image links. Fix anything it lists, then open the website.

Customers who had items in their cart before the change see them marked **"Please choose a colour"**. If a product has only one colour, the item is converted automatically. Otherwise the customer picks the colour (or removes the item) before checking out. The store never guesses a colour.

## Cloudflare frontend

The same storefront can be served from Cloudflare as a Worker with static assets (first on a temporary `*.workers.dev` address), with this Apps Script project and spreadsheet staying the backend. The Worker calls the web app server-side:

- `GET /api/config` → `doGet?action=config`
- `GET /api/products` → `doGet?action=products`
- `POST /api/orders` → `doPost` (with `WEBHOOK_SECRET`)

The static files are generated from `src/` by `tools/build_cloudflare.mjs` (run automatically by `wrangler deploy`), so both sites stay identical. Setup, environment variables, deployment and rollback are in **[CLOUDFLARE.md](CLOUDFLARE.md)**.

### Calling the web app from elsewhere

Things to know when anything other than the Apps Script page calls the `/exec` URL:

- **Redirect.** Every response is a 302 to a one-time `script.googleusercontent.com` link that holds the output. By then the script has already run. Read the result with a GET and never re-send a POST. The Cloudflare Worker follows it by hand, and only to Google hosts.
- **No status codes or headers.** The HTTP status is 200 even for failures, and `doPost` can't read request headers. Success and errors are in the JSON body (`success`, `code`). Google error and sign-in pages come back as HTML, so an answer that isn't JSON is never a success.
- **Body format.** `doPost` reads `e.postData.contents`. Send JSON as `text/plain`.
- **Access must be "Anyone".** Otherwise requests are redirected to a Google sign-in page.
- **Secrets belong on servers.** `WEBHOOK_SECRET` must never be put in browser code. The Cloudflare site keeps it in a Cloudflare secret.
- **Quotas still apply** (script runtime, simultaneous executions, email).

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause and fix |
|---|---|
| **"Sorry, unable to open the file at this time"** or a Google login page for customers | Deployment access isn't **Anyone**. Edit the deployment (Manage deployments → pencil), or create one with **Who has access: Anyone**. Workspace admins may block this. |
| **"Script function not found: doGet"** | `Code.gs` is missing or wasn't saved. Save all files and create a **new version** of the deployment. |
| Customers see an old version of the site | You saved code but didn't publish a new version: Manage deployments → Edit → **New version** → Deploy. |
| **"We couldn't load the products"** | Open **Executions** in the Apps Script editor for the real error. Usually: the `Products` tab is missing or renamed, a header is misspelled, or the script isn't bound to the spreadsheet and `SPREADSHEET_ID` isn't set. Run `setupSpreadsheet` and then `checkCatalog`. |
| **"The "Orders" tab is missing…"** or orders fail with a header error | Recreate or rename the tab to `Orders` with the exact 9 headers, or run `setupSpreadsheet`. |
| **"We couldn't load the products"** right after updating the code | The `Products` tab is still in the old format. See [Migrating the Products tab](#migrating-the-products-tab). |
| A product doesn't appear | Run `checkCatalog`. Usually its rows disagree on Name, Description, Price or Category, or the price/name is missing. |
| A colour doesn't appear | The same Color appears twice for that Product ID, or the row has no Product ID/Color. Run `checkCatalog`. |
| A colour or size shows as unavailable | Its size cells aren't exactly `1`. Empty, `0`, `2`, `yes` and checkboxes all count as unavailable. |
| Placeholder image instead of the photo | The link isn't public (Drive: share with **Anyone with the link**), isn't `https://`, or points to a web page instead of an image. Open the link in a private window to check. |
| **"Authorization is required"** / permission errors after editing code | You added a feature that needs a new permission. Run any function (e.g. `checkCatalog`) in the editor to re-authorize, then deploy a new version. |
| **No order emails** | Check `NOTIFICATION_EMAIL`, your spam folder, and the **note on the Order ID cell** of the order (hover over it). Run `sendTestEmail` to check delivery and quota. |
| **"Service invoked too many times for one day: email"** | The daily `MailApp` quota is used up: about **100 recipients/day** for free Gmail accounts, about **1,500/day** for Google Workspace. Orders are **still saved**; only the email is skipped (noted on the Order ID cell). The quota resets about 24 hours after first use. |
| **"The store is busy right now"** | Many orders at once and the lock wasn't free within 30 seconds. Nothing was saved, and the customer can safely retry. |
| Order ID shows as `4729` instead of `04729` | Someone typed over the cell or changed the column format. Run `setupSpreadsheet` to reset column B to plain text. New orders always write the ID as text. |
| Cart empties unexpectedly | The browser blocks or clears site storage (private mode, strict privacy settings, Safari storage limits). The cart still works within the visit. |

## Known limitations

- **Hosting banner.** Free Gmail-hosted web apps show Google's notice *"This application was created by a Google Apps Script user"* above the page. It goes away with a Workspace account or after moving the frontend to your own domain.
- **iframe.** Apps Script serves the page inside an iframe from `*.googleusercontent.com`. Browser back/forward doesn't move between shop, checkout and thank-you views, and the address bar always shows the `/exec` URL.
- **Cart storage** belongs to the Apps Script origin and to that browser. It doesn't sync across devices, and very strict privacy settings may reset it.
- **Duplicate protection is best effort.** CacheService entries can be evicted before 6 hours. In that rare case, a retry long after a lost response could create a second order. Customers are told to contact you instead of re-ordering if problems persist.
- **Stock is on/off, not a quantity, and is never updated automatically.** A size cell of `1` means "available", however many pieces you have. Reservations don't change it, so several customers can reserve the last piece until you set the cell to `0`. Reservations are confirmed by email, so you can resolve this then.
- **Rate limits use hourly buckets** in CacheService. They're approximate and reset at the top of each hour.
- **Totals are product subtotals.** Delivery is arranged by email, as stated on the checkout page and in the confirmation.
- **Throughput.** Orders are processed one at a time under the lock, which is fine for a small shop (dozens of orders per minute). Apps Script also limits simultaneous executions per account.
- **Sorting and pagination**: all products load at once, which is fine for a few hundred products.
